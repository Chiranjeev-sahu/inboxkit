# Ten-candidate sourcing pilot

**Historical experiment, 15 October 2026.** Its counts, fit/signal restrictions and email-policy interpretations describe that test, not the current export policy. Use [decisions](decisions.md), the [current runbook](../skills/lead-research/SKILL.md), and [current campaign outputs](../README.md#review-the-campaign) for present guidance.

Observed on 15 October 2026. This is an exploratory sample, not the assignment's final 100-row list. No outreach was sent. The two signal groups were chosen before searching: five hiring candidates and five client case study candidates. A company is a candidate only when its public materials indicate that it runs managed cold email for clients. A signal is recorded separately from that fit judgment.

## Results

| Measure | Hiring | Client case study | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Candidate agencies researched | 5 | 5 | 10 |
| Named buyers and work emails found | 5 | 5 | 10 |
| MillionVerifier `ok` / `good` | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| MillionVerifier `catch_all` / `risky` | 4 | 4 | 8 |

An email found by a provider is **not** automatically send-ready. For this pilot, `ok` / `good` is the clear valid bucket. The eight catch-all results remain uncertain. A second check on two catch-all addresses with Reoon returned `catch_all` and `is_safe_to_send=false` both times, so it did not resolve that uncertainty. The work emails and verification statuses below are for research and review, not a permission to send.

The sample does not establish which signal will yield 100 qualified rows. Hiring has direct operational relevance, but several discovered posts lack a reliable publication date or are closed. Named client case studies are easier to cite, but a published result alone does not prove a current infrastructure purchase. The main measured bottleneck in this sample was clear email verification: 2 of 10.

## Provider observations

- A connected **Lusha MCP** worked in Codex. A batch contact search and one email reveal consumed 2 of 40 free credits; 38 remained after the pilot. It found six contact records, while one was not found and three were restricted. It supplied Zaid's address.
- First-party agency pages supplied several public work addresses. Hunter's email finder supplied Mark's address with a valid result. Findymail supplied Leon's, Alexander's, and Jay's addresses. A Prospeo batch request returned HTTP 429, so the waterfall moved on. This is an access/rate-limit observation, not evidence that Prospeo has no usable credits.
- MillionVerifier returned two `ok` / `good` and eight `catch_all` / `risky` results. Reoon returned catch-all for two sampled addresses. Different finder and verifier labels should not be collapsed into one “verified” flag.
- Exa, Firecrawl, Tavily, Apollo, ZeroBounce, and other available credits were not exhausted or integrated merely to exercise them. The waterfall stopped when a usable lookup result had been found. Search and page reading used Codex's web research tools; the only connected provider MCP tested here was Lusha. No MCP configuration or application code was created.

## What this changes

1. Keep hiring and client case studies as **candidate** signals. This ten-row convenience sample is too small and too uneven to select a winner, especially with ambiguous job dates and two weak case-study recency claims.
2. Before scaling, set an explicit final-sheet policy for catch-all addresses. If the target is 100 *clear-valid* work emails, this sample suggests that 100 researched candidates will not suffice. If the brief allows uncertain statuses in the 100 rows, label them and keep a separate send-ready subset.
3. Tighten the first-pass signal test: require a dated, relevant job or dated, named client case, and record how many agency candidates fail that rule. Do not quietly convert an undated job or an evergreen case to a recent buying event.
4. MCPs are useful provider interfaces, but this pilot does not justify configuring every provider as an MCP. Codex already operated the connected Lusha MCP and direct provider APIs. The clean-checkout workflow still needs a documented way to access whichever tools it actually uses.
